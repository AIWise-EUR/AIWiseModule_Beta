Formulating a Central Research Question

In this step, you move from exploring your topic to formulating possible central research questions. Based on the directions you identified earlier, try to formulate two or three candidate questions. The sections below explain what a central research question is and provide criteria to assess whether your question is suitable for a literature review. Once you have a candidate question, discuss it with your tutor or course coordinator to confirm that it fits the assignment and the course expectations.

The links below lead to course materials related to this stage. Please read them before using the GenAI activity on this page, since the activity assumes that you are already familiar with the basic content. A basic understanding is also necessary to use GenAI appropriately and critically.

Using GenAI in this stage

For this stage, we strongly recommend formulating your central research question without GenAI.

Your central research question should reflect your own interests, perspective, and reasoning about your assigned topic. There is no statistically "best" central question. A question may sound well written, but still be a poor fit for your assignment, your assigned subject, or your own motivation.

If you need support, discuss your ideas with your classmates, tutor, or course coordinator. They are familiar with the expectations of the assignment and can best judge whether your question is suitable, clear, and feasible within the scope of this course.

If you still choose to use GenAI, use it only for process support. For example, you may ask it to help you clarify your wording or to challenge your reasoning. Do not copy a generated research question and treat it as your own. Always verify your question with the course guidelines and your tutor.
